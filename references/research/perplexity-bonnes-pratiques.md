**The strongest feedback tools make it easy to contribute, preserve who asked and why, and reliably report what happened next.** Votes are useful demand signals—not binding commitments or a representative poll.

*As of November 16, 2026, Hellonext’s current name is **FeatureOS**, not Zenu; the company announced the rebrand in 2023.* [web:300]

### Best practices and features

| Topic | Best practice and concrete evidence |
|---|---|
| **Widget placement** | Put a persistent, clearly labeled **Feedback** button in the signed-in app shell or help menu, plus contextual links beside complex workflows or error states. A corner launcher is convenient only if it does not cover chat, save buttons, or mobile navigation. Test real breakpoints. [web:328][web:330] |
| **Minimize friction** | Open a non-blocking panel in place; let people search and vote in one click, or submit a short description with optional context. Avoid mandatory surveys and interruptions during checkout or form entry. If actively prompting, do it after a natural stopping point, cap frequency, and allow dismissal. Support keyboard navigation, accessible labels, and focus return. [web:326][web:328] |
| **Identity without another account** | For signed-in customers, issue a **short-lived JWT on your server** from the authenticated session and pass it to the widget. Keep the signing secret server-side; never trust a browser-supplied identity. Canny supports this for its widget, and Nolt supports JWT identification for its modal widget. Productboard’s embedded portal also accepts JWTs; its tokens require `iat` and expire after 12 hours at most. [web:64][web:209][web:239] |
| **Passwordless and guests** | For visitors outside the app, offer a magic link or email verification where identity and follow-up matter; Upvoty documents magic links, and Productboard verifies portal feedback by email. Alternatively, permit guests—but distinguish **an email address supplied by a guest** from a verified identity. Anonymous cookie-based voting is lower friction but weaker for deduplication and follow-up. [web:224][web:240][web:341] |
| **Guest-notification trade-off** | Do not promise every guest an update. Featurebase explicitly says anonymous guest votes do **not** create identified email subscribers; users must sign in or be identified for email updates. Offer an optional verified email or follow action, and test the actual flow. [web:226] |
| **Duplicate detection** | Search as the user types, show similar requests with a clear “vote instead” action, and use semantic matching for synonyms. Let a person submit a genuinely different request anyway. FeatureOS suggests related posts; Featurebase’s Autopilot can link supporting feedback and suggest or apply merges. [web:377][web:415] |
| **Merging rules** | Merge only when requests express the **same underlying need and outcome**, not merely the same screen or keyword. Choose a recognizable canonical title; preserve source wording, unique voters, comments, subscribers, links, and an audit trail. Provide redirect/unmerge where available and review AI merges. Canny combines votes and comments without notifying users on merge; Sleekplan deduplicates voters but warns that **unmerging does not move transferred votes, comments, or subscribers back**. Test these behaviors before bulk cleanup. [web:77][web:121][web:142] |
| **Value and segment weighting** | Keep **raw people, distinct accounts, segment counts, and revenue exposure** visible separately. Join voters to current plan, account, and MRR/ARR; count account revenue once per request even if several colleagues vote. Canny’s MRR sort does this; Productboard’s segment score incorporates how important linked feedback is to companies. Revenue attached to a request is **exposure**, not proof of incremental revenue or causation. [web:16][web:2] |
| **A practical weighted view** | For example, compare: 20 users from one $2,000-MRR account versus 20 users from 20 $100-MRR accounts. Both may represent $2,000 MRR, but they have very different account concentration and reach. Review both alongside strategic fit, urgency, implementation effort, churn risk, and underserved segments—not a single weighted total. [web:15][web:402] |
| **RICE prioritization** | Use **RICE = Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort**. Define reach over the same period for every idea, use confidence as a fraction, and estimate effort across product, design, and engineering. Example: 450 customers per quarter × 3 impact × 1.0 confidence ÷ 2 person-months = **675**. It is a comparative estimate, not an automatic decision. [web:164][web:165] |
| **Trending and Canny sorts** | Offer **Top**, **Trending**, and **Newest**, rather than treating one ranking as truth. Canny defines Top as raw votes, Trending as votes received recently, and MRR as associated company revenue; it also provides newest and oldest sorts. Its documentation reviewed here does **not** specify an exact Trending decay formula. Trending on the public portal is distinct from Canny Ideas’ internal prioritization scores. [web:16][web:311] |
| **Example hot score—not Canny’s** | A custom implementation could use `Σ 0.5^(vote age in days / 30)`: each vote contributes 1 initially and 0.5 after 30 days. Publish the half-life and still expose raw counts; tune it against actual board behavior. This is an illustrative time-decay design, not a claim about any vendor’s algorithm. |
| **Canny prioritization score** | In current **Canny Ideas**, the documented score is `Σ(weight × impact value) ÷ Σ(weight × cost value)`; if cost totals zero, Canny uses the impact sum instead. Its **legacy internal roadmap** uses a different normalized formula. Neither should be confused with public Trending or automatically assumed to be multiplicative RICE. [web:318] |
| **Support tickets and vote-on-behalf** | Let support, sales, and success attach a real customer to an existing request from the ticket or call—not cast repeated votes as themselves. Record the customer’s words, source link, date, and urgency; deduplicate by person and account. Canny’s Zendesk integration links the ticket and adds the requester’s vote. UserVoice’s Zendesk sidebar similarly captures feedback with the requester’s email. [web:275][web:111] |
| **Internal comments** | Keep deal context, investigation notes, estimates, and decision rationale in explicitly private, permission-controlled comments; keep customer-facing explanations separate. Canny’s internal comments are admin-only and paid; Featurebase distinguishes private comments from public replies. Verify visibility before posting customer-specific information. [web:270][web:430] |
| **Moderation and anti-spam** | Use one vote per identified person per request, rate limits, bot detection, abuse reporting, ban controls, and an optional approval queue. Apply stronger verification or CAPTCHA when abuse warrants it; do not silently delete legitimate criticism. Nolt offers bot protection, manual review, profanity controls, and bans; Upvoty offers an approval queue that can merge duplicates before publication. [web:215][web:139] |
| **Roadmap presentation** | Show a curated, understandable **Under review / Planned / In progress / Shipped** view linked to request threads. Separate public intent from internal delivery plans, use broad time horizons rather than speculative dates, and explain that votes inform rather than guarantee delivery. UserVoice supports separate internal and public roadmaps; its roadmap features can roll up evidence from several ideas. [web:119] |
| **Close the loop** | On a meaningful decision or release, send an update to **the people who asked**, explaining what changed, availability, and a link to instructions; publish a changelog for the broader audience. Respect access and unsubscribe settings. Do not assume publishing release notes notifies voters: Canny’s changelog emails go to **changelog subscribers**, while status updates can separately notify voters and internally tracked users. Featurebase likewise distinguishes release-update subscribers from request-status emails. [web:284][web:285][web:295] |
| **AI in 2025–2026** | Use AI to extract requests from support, Slack, email, and calls; categorize, summarize, identify themes, and suggest links or duplicates. Preserve original quotes and source links, show confidence/uncertainty, audit changes, and keep humans responsible for merges and roadmap decisions. Featurebase’s September 2026 Feedback 2.0 separates **Requests** from supporting **Insights** and offers off, assisted, and automatic modes. Productboard Spark synthesizes connected feedback into themes and evidence-linked opportunities. [web:415][web:417][web:356] |
| **Privacy: GDPR** | Document the processing purpose and appropriate lawful basis; give clear notice, minimize identity and behavioral data, set retention/deletion rules, and support applicable access and erasure rights. Use processor contracts and assess international-transfer safeguards where applicable. Treat screenshots, session replays, support transcripts, and AI subprocessors as separate data flows—not harmless widget metadata. [web:31][web:446] |
| **Privacy: Québec Law 25** | For applicable organizations, conduct a privacy impact assessment for qualifying acquisition, development, or overhaul of a personal-information system. Before communicating information **outside Québec—including to another Canadian province—or entrusting it to an outside-Québec provider**, assess adequate protection and use a written agreement reflecting the assessment. Map widget vendors, hosting, support access, and AI subprocessors. [web:257][web:261] |
| **Bias, stale boards, and expectations** | Vote totals are self-selected and affected by exposure and bandwagon effects. Compare voter composition with the active customer base; sample quieter segments and interview users about problems. Rotate or diversify discovery and hide counts where practical. Assign an owner and weekly triage cadence; explain declines and revisit old items. Track response time, unresolved age, segment coverage, and the share of requesters actually notified on release. [web:402][web:382] |

### What the named products illustrate

| Tool | Distinctive, documented example |
|---|---|
| **Canny** | JWT/Identify, revenue-aware sorting, vote-on-behalf, and Autopilot capture from support and sales sources. Its changelog-subscriber and voter-status notifications are separate mechanisms. [web:64][web:46][web:285] |
| **Featurebase** | Requests retain individual supporting Insights; Autopilot can organize and deduplicate them at configurable automation levels. Its guest actions are separately configurable, but unidentified guests do not receive request email updates. [web:415][web:226] |
| **Frill** | One widget offers popover, modal, sidebar, and inline styles; it supports JWT identification, roadmap announcements, and a benefit/cost matrix with revenue weighting. Its documentation describes both guest authentication and more restrictive guest voting configurations, so test the intended mode. [web:183][web:180][web:342][web:348] |
| **Nolt** | Embedded modal board/roadmap with JWT SSO, anonymous participation options, merge tools, vote-on-behalf, manual moderation, and bot protection. [web:209][web:210][web:215] |
| **UserVoice** | Account- and revenue-aware feedback, importance ratings, support-ticket capture, and separate public/internal roadmaps are especially relevant to account-based product teams. [web:107][web:111][web:119] |
| **Productboard** | A curated portal collects importance ratings and private feedback rather than exposing every customer submission as a public discussion; its customer-importance and segment views combine feedback with account context. [web:240][web:242][web:2] |
| **Upvoty** | Combines in-app boards, moderation, Merge AI, roadmap and changelog; it documents passwordless email magic links and JWT SSO. [web:144][web:139][web:224] |
| **Sleekplan** | Combines feedback, roadmap and changelog in a widget, with guest or signed-token identity and internal discussions. Its documented merge/unmerge limitation is important for operational testing. [web:124][web:125][web:121] |
| **Userback** | Particularly useful for visual bug and usability feedback: annotated screenshots, browser context, and—on applicable plans—session replay, alongside a separate feature-voting portal. These richer captures require extra privacy care. [web:91][web:96] |
| **Hellonext / FeatureOS** | The renamed product combines boards, roadmap, changelog, forms and knowledge base. Its AI offers sentiment, summaries, related-post matching and merge suggestions; the vendor says a person chooses the parent for AI-enhanced duplicate merges. [web:300][web:371] |
| **UserJot** | Combines board, roadmap, changelog and chat in one widget. Public-board guests can participate without an account, while its widget documentation says voting and commenting require identification; its Juno chat flow asks the customer to confirm before filing or voting on their behalf. [web:194][web:208] |

**Selection check:** capabilities, automation defaults, notification recipients, and plan gates change. Before purchase, test one duplicate merge, one support-ticket vote, one guest-to-identified journey, one private note, and one shipped-status notification end to end.
